Privacy Policy

Last updated: 26 October 2026

Overview

Middle-earth Explorer ("the app") is an unofficial fan app for iPhone and iPad made by one independent developer, Alp Ozcan ("I", "me"). This policy covers version 3.2 of the app. It explains what data the app handles, who receives it, why, how long it is kept and how you can say no.

The short version:

  • The app works without an account. You can optionally create one, only to get the emails you choose. The app shows no ads of its own. YouTube's player, which plays the video clips, may show YouTube's ads.
  • It never asks for your name, phone number or contacts. It asks for an email address only if you choose to create an account, and with Sign in with Apple you can hide your real address.
  • News alerts are optional and off until you turn them on. If you do, my news server keeps what it needs to send them, as described under "News and News Alerts".
  • It does not track you across other companies' apps or websites, and it does not sell data.
  • It sends usage analytics and crash reports tied to a random ID, so I can see what works and fix bugs. Both are on by default, and you can turn each one off in Profile > Privacy.
  • Some features ask outside services for content, such as film details, book covers or live events. Those requests go straight from your device to that service.

Who Is Responsible

The app is made and run by Alp Ozcan, who is responsible for the data described here. You can reach me at hi@alp.me about anything in this policy.

Data That Stays on Your Device

Most of what you do in the app never leaves your device:

  • Bookmarks, recent searches, Spoiler Shield settings, quiz results, Daily Riddle progress and streaks, New Zealand filming trail check-ins and trip plans, your home city for Events, your app icon choice and your privacy choices are stored on your device.
  • Search runs on your device. When analytics is on, the app records only how many results a search returned, never what you typed.
  • Reminders (Season 3 episodes, the Daily Riddle, On this day, event reminders and city alerts) are local notifications. Your device schedules and shows them; no server is involved. The app asks for notification permission only when you turn a reminder on. News alerts are different: they come from my news server, as described under "News and News Alerts".
  • Widgets read the same on-device data through an app group on your device.
  • Places and characters can appear in Search (Spotlight) on your iPhone or iPad. That index lives on your device.
  • If you save your quiz result card, the app asks for permission to add it to your photo library. This is the only thing the app saves to Photos. It can add photos but cannot see or read your library.
  • If you add an event to your calendar, the app asks for write-only calendar access. It can add the event but cannot read your calendar.
  • If you use "Near me" or "Check in" on the New Zealand filming trail, your approximate location is used on your device to sort sites by distance and confirm a visit. It is not sent anywhere.
  • A cached copy of your Fellowship+ status is stored in your device's Keychain.

Optional Account and Emails

You can use every part of the app without an account. If you choose to create one in Profile > Account, you sign in with Apple or, where the app offers them, with Google, Microsoft or Facebook, or with a one-time code sent to your email address. There is no password. The app shows Google's, Microsoft's or Facebook's own sign-in page in a private browser window; it never sees your password there.

What I keep:

  • your email address, or the private relay address Apple creates if you choose Hide My Email;
  • an account ID created for you, and the user ID that Apple, Google, Microsoft or Facebook gives this app if you signed in with them, and whether they confirmed your email address;
  • if you signed in with Apple or Google: a token, stored encrypted, used only to withdraw the app's access with them when you delete your account;
  • your app language and time zone, so emails arrive in your language and at a sensible hour;
  • which emails you turned on (event reminders, product news), with the exact wording you saw and the time, so I can prove your choice;
  • only while event reminders are on: the events you asked to be reminded about (title, venue, city, date and link);
  • when you signed in, and a sign-in session tied to your copy of the app;
  • if you sign in with an email code: for up to one day, a keyed hash of the code (never the code itself) and, to stop abuse, keyed hashes of your email address and IP address.

I never ask Apple for your name. Google and Microsoft include your name in their basic sign-in, and Facebook in your public profile; I keep neither your name nor your picture, only your email address and their user ID.

Why, and on what basis: to run your account (contract), and to send the emails you turned on (your consent, which you can withdraw at any time). Product news is only sent after you confirm it from a first email, unless you just proved the address with a code. Creating an account is not consent to any email.

Emails are not sent yet. In version 3.2 the server saves your choices but sends no emails. Before the first email goes out, this policy will name the company that delivers them. The emails will contain no open or click tracking.

Who processes it: Fly.io (servers in Amsterdam, the Netherlands), Cloudflare R2 (encrypted backups in the EU) and Apple (Sign in with Apple). If you choose Google, Microsoft or Facebook, that company handles the sign-in under its own privacy policy and learns that you signed in to Middle-earth Explorer.

How long: until you delete your account. Sign-in sessions end 180 days after last use, when you sign out, or when you delete the account. Event details are deleted 7 days after the event, or when you turn event reminders off. Sign-in codes work for 10 minutes and are deleted after a day. Deleted data leaves the encrypted backups within 30 days.

Deleting your account: in the app, Profile > Account > Delete account, or by writing to hi@alp.me. This deletes your account, your email address, your choices and the events you shared. It also withdraws the app's access with Facebook, and with Apple (Sign in with Apple) and Google when a token was kept. Microsoft offers apps no way to do this: remove Middle-earth Explorer at account.live.com/consent/Manage (or, for a work or school account, in My Apps). What the app keeps on your device (progress, bookmarks, follows) stays on your device. Deleting your account does not turn off news alerts, which are not tied to an account; turn them off in Profile > Notifications > News alerts.

Stopping emails: turn the topic off in Profile > Account, or use the unsubscribe link in any email. Signing out of the app does not stop the emails; turning them off or deleting the account does.

Emails are only for people aged 16 or over.

iCloud Sync of Journey Progress (Fellowship+)

If you subscribe to Fellowship+, your Journey Order progress (which films, series and books you have marked) syncs between your devices through your own iCloud account, using Apple's iCloud key-value storage. I cannot see this data. Apple stores it under its own privacy policy. If Fellowship+ ends, nothing more is synced, and nothing is deleted from your devices. You can remove the synced copy by turning off iCloud for the app in iOS Settings.

Usage Analytics (TelemetryDeck)

What: When "Share usage analytics" is on, the app sends TelemetryDeck short signals such as "a screen was viewed", "a map marker was tapped", "the paywall was shown" or "a purchase finished", with a few details like the screen or content type, the app version, iOS version, device model, language and region, and which App Store product page you installed from. Purchase signals include the plan (monthly or yearly), never payment details.

Identifier: Signals carry a random identifier created for this app on your device. TelemetryDeck hashes it again before storing it. It is not your Apple ID, your advertising identifier or anything that names you.

Why: To learn which features people use and where the app is confusing, so I can improve it. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in running and improving the app. You can object at any time with the switch.

Where: TelemetryDeck is based in Germany and stores data in the EU.

Statsig (version 3.0 only): Version 3.0 uses Statsig, based in the United States, to run a short map performance test. Statsig receives a random, app-scoped identifier, which map renderer your device was assigned, and map speed and memory measurements. It never receives your location or anything that names you. In version 3.0 the "Share usage analytics" switch does not stop it. Version 3.1 removes Statsig from the app, so updating the app stops it.

How to stop: Turn off Profile > Privacy > Share usage analytics. TelemetryDeck stops at once and is not started at the next launch. The same switch also stops purchase records to RevenueCat: the app stops reporting purchases at once, and RevenueCat stops completely the next time the app starts (see below).

Crash Reports (Sentry)

What: When "Send crash reports" is on and the app crashes or hits an unexpected error, it sends Sentry a technical report: the error and stack trace, the device model, iOS or iPadOS version, app version, memory and performance measurements, and a short trail of the screens and actions just before the problem (for example "opened the Events tab"). The trail never includes what you typed, your location or your name. When a third-party service the app reads from (such as an event listings or film database service) refuses a request, the report also includes the first 64 characters of that service's error message, for example "Access denied", with any keys, tokens and web addresses removed. Reports carry a random installation identifier.

When the switch is on, the app also sends Sentry performance measurements for about one in five screen visits: how long the screen takes to appear, how smoothly it draws (slow and frozen frames) and how long the app took to start. These carry the screen's name and timings only, never what you typed, your location or your name. If the app stops responding for two seconds or more, it sends a report like a crash report, so I can find what blocked it.

Why: To find and fix crashes. The legal basis is my legitimate interest in keeping the app working. You can object at any time with the switch.

Where: Sentry stores these reports in its EU region (Germany).

How to stop: Turn off Profile > Privacy > Send crash reports. Sentry stops at once and is not started at the next launch.

Live Events and Your Location (Ticketmaster)

The Events tab shows concerts and screenings near a city.

  • If you pick a city, the app sends that city's coordinates to Ticketmaster's Discovery API to find events nearby.
  • If you allow location access, the app asks iOS for your approximate location only (reduced accuracy, roughly city level) and sends those coordinates to Ticketmaster instead. The app asks for location only when you use this feature, and only while you are using the app.

Ticketmaster receives the request under its own privacy policy. The app does not store your location history and does not send your location to analytics or crash reports.

Content From Other Services

To show films, books, clips and lore, the app downloads content from these services. Each request goes from your device straight to the service, so the service sees your IP address and the item requested, as any website would. The app sends no identifier of its own with these requests.

  • TMDB (themoviedb.org): film and TV details and images.
  • YouTube (Google): video clips, their details and thumbnails. Clips play in YouTube's official embedded player, loaded from youtube-nocookie.com (YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode). YouTube runs that player under its own terms and privacy policy, and it may set cookies, show YouTube's ads and collect data once a clip plays. The app keeps the player's storage in memory only, so it is cleared when the app quits. This app uses YouTube API Services: by watching clips you agree to the YouTube Terms of Service, and YouTube's handling of your data is described in the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Open Library (Internet Archive): book covers.
  • The One API (the-one-api.dev): character and quote data.
  • The One Wiki to Rule Them All (lotr.fandom.com, run by Fandom): character text and images.

Each service handles these requests under its own privacy policy.

Purchases (Apple)

Fellowship+ is sold through the App Store using Apple's StoreKit. Apple handles the payment and your Apple ID. I never see your name, email or payment details. The app asks Apple whether you have an active subscription so it can unlock features. With analytics on, the app records that a purchase started, finished, failed or was restored, and which plan it was.

Purchase Records (RevenueCat)

What: From version 3.1, when "Share usage analytics" is on, the app tells RevenueCat about your Fellowship+ purchases after Apple has completed them: the plan, its price and currency, the purchase and renewal dates, and Apple's signed record of the transaction. RevenueCat also receives a random identifier it creates for this app, Apple's identifier for vendors (identifierForVendor: an identifier Apple gives my apps on your device, which changes when you remove all of them; it is not the advertising identifier), the app and iOS versions, and your approximate country from your IP address. It never receives your name, email, Apple ID or payment details: Apple handles the payment.

Why: To see subscription statistics such as renewals, cancellations and trial conversions. RevenueCat never decides what the app unlocks; that still comes from Apple.

Where: RevenueCat, Inc., based in the United States. RevenueCat keeps these records for as long as this app uses its service; write to me to have yours deleted.

How to stop: Turn off "Share usage analytics" in Profile > Privacy. The app itself stops reporting purchases straight away. RevenueCat stops completely the next time the app starts: until you close the app, RevenueCat's code that is already running can still pass on purchase updates it sees from Apple, such as a renewal or an Ask to Buy approval.

Ticket Links and Affiliate Commissions

The app never sells tickets. "See on" buttons open the seller's own page in a Safari view inside the app, and some of these links earn me a small commission when you buy tickets. Ticketmaster links carry three labels: the screen you tapped from, the event and the app version. Other ticket links may open through Skimlinks, which redirects to the ticket site and records the click so the seller can pay a commission. What happens on those pages is handled by Skimlinks and the seller under their own privacy policies; the app cannot see it and sends Skimlinks nothing about you.

News and News Alerts

From version 3.2 the app has a News page and optional news alerts. News alerts are off until you turn on at least one news topic in Profile > Notifications > News alerts, or from the card on the News page. The app asks for notification permission only then.

Reading the News page: the app asks my news server for the latest stories. Before the first request, the app uses Apple's App Attest to prove that the request comes from a genuine copy of the app. The server then keeps the App Attest key ID and public key for your copy of the app, to check later requests. App Attest does not identify you, and the key is deleted 30 days after it was last used, unless news alerts or an account still use it. These requests carry no other information about you, and the server does not store your IP address.

What the server keeps when you turn news alerts on:

  • the push notification token Apple issues for this app on your device;
  • the App Attest key ID of your copy of the app;
  • your app language and time zone;
  • the news topics you chose, your Spoiler Shield level for news, your quiet hours and how many alerts a week you want;
  • the app version, and when your device last checked in;
  • a record of which alerts were sent to your device, kept for 90 days so I can limit how many you receive and never send the same story twice.

I use this data only to decide which news alerts to send you and when. The server does not collect your name, email address, location or advertising identifier, does not link this data to an account, does not store IP addresses and does not use this data for advertising or tracking.

Delivery: alerts are delivered by Apple's Push Notification service. My server sends Apple the push token and the alert's text; Apple handles this under its own privacy policy.

How long, and how to delete it: turning every news topic off deletes your device's registration from the server. Turning off notifications for the app in iOS Settings does the same the next time you open the app. If Apple reports that the token is no longer valid, or you do not open the app for 180 days, the registration is deleted too. In rare cases, for example after reinstalling the app, the server cannot confirm that the request comes from the copy of the app that registered; it then stops all alerts to that device at once and deletes the registration within 180 days. Deleting the app stops alerts at once, and the registration is deleted when Apple reports the token as invalid or after 180 days.

Where: the server is hosted by Fly.io in the EU (Amsterdam), with encrypted backups stored with Cloudflare R2 in the EU. Backups are kept for 30 days, so a deleted registration can remain in an encrypted backup for up to 30 days before it is removed. Fly.io handles network traffic under its own privacy policy.

AI-assisted summaries: news headlines, summaries and alert texts in the app are written with the help of an AI model (Claude, by Anthropic, used through OpenRouter) from public headlines and feed descriptions, and may contain mistakes. Every item links to its original source. No data about you is sent to the AI model.

What the App Does Not Do

  • It does not use the advertising identifier (IDFA) and does not ask to track you.
  • Purchase records never include the advertising identifier (IDFA). They do include Apple's identifier for vendors, as described under "Purchase Records (RevenueCat)".
  • It does not show ads of its own or share data with advertisers or data brokers. YouTube's embedded player may show YouTube's ads, as described under "Content From Other Services".
  • It does not sell or rent data.
  • It does not collect precise location, contacts, photos, health data or anything you type, except the email address you enter if you choose to sign up with an email code.

Your Choices and Rights

  • Profile > Privacy has the analytics and crash-report switches, the list of services the app uses and a link to iOS Settings.
  • Profile > Notifications > News alerts turns news alerts on or off, topic by topic.
  • Profile > Account lets you choose your emails, sign out or delete your account.
  • iOS Settings > Middle Earth lets you change location, notification, photo and calendar access at any time.
  • Deleting the app removes everything stored on your device. Synced Journey progress stays in your iCloud until you remove it there.

If you are in the EU, the UK or a place with similar laws, you can ask to access, correct or delete data about you, object to its use or complain to your data protection authority. If you have an account, write to hi@alp.me from its email address (or tell me the address) and I will answer within 30 days. Without an account, I usually cannot link data to you, but I will do what I can.

Children's Privacy

The app is not directed to children under 13 and does not knowingly collect personal information from them. Emails from the optional account are only for people aged 16 or over.
